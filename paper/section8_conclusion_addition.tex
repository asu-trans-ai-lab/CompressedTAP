Section~\ref{sec:fw_origin} elevates this representation from an implementation alternative to a methodological statement with four conclusions of its own. First, the latent route-family construction is convexity preserving (Proposition~\ref{prop:convexity-preservation}): each latent column is a convex combination of feasible path columns, so the compressed problem is a convex program over OD demand simplices whose solutions reconstruct feasible path flows exactly, and promotion only enlarges a nested family of convex inner approximations. Second, the compression gain is a property of the representation layer rather than of any one algorithm: the same latent atoms, gradient, and duality-gap certificate drive latent Frank--Wolfe, the latent projected gradient, and latent mirror descent, and the recorded matched-accuracy speedups grow with the pool-to-atom ratio ($7.7\times$ for the projected gradient at $K=512$ in the recorded sweep, with both operators gaining further at the $K=2048$ space-time regime) --- while wall-time and kernel-level evidence must be kept separate, since Frank--Wolfe's sublinear tail can mask per-iteration gains. Third, the explicit major columns are not optional: pure latent compression without an anchor fails by $149.8\%$ on the congested Chicago instance, and a single explicit major per OD repairs this to $0.53\%$, confirming that the support mechanism identified in the controlled experiments governs the latent geometry as well. Fourth, the construction is not specific to traffic assignment: the same $x = Wz$ representation reproduces the identical pattern --- latent-only compression loses a few percent, majors plus latent atoms recover the optimum at several-fold column reduction --- on cutting-stock and unit-commitment column pools, suggesting that the major--latent principle applies to column-pool convex programs in general.
