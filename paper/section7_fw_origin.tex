\section{Convexity Preservation and Solver Portability of the Latent Representation}
\label{sec:fw_origin}

The preceding sections establish the latent representation inside one
solver family. This section establishes, first, that the representation
is exact convex programming rather than heuristic grouping, and second,
that it is not tied to any one solver. The grouped construction of
Section~\ref{sec:grouped} is restated as a feasible \emph{atom}
geometry, two further first-order methods --- Frank--Wolfe and a latent
projected gradient --- are run on that geometry unchanged, and the
speedup is measured under a matched-accuracy protocol across route
richness up to the scale at which space-time columns live. The finding
is stated at the outset because the experiments below are designed only
to test it: \emph{the compression gain is a property of the
representation layer, portable across linear-oracle,
Euclidean-projection, and entropic update operators, and it grows with
the ratio of route richness to latent dimension.}

\subsection{Latent Route Families as Convex Combinations}
\label{sec:latent-convex}

Consider OD pair $i$ and let $b_p \in \Re^m$ denote the $p$th row of the
path-link incidence matrix $B$, written as a column vector. As in
Section~\ref{sec:grouped}, partition the minor paths of OD pair $i$ into
groups $G_{ik}$, $k = 1, \dots, r_i$, with fixed shares satisfying
\eqref{eq:group-shares}:
$\pi_{ikp} \ge 0$ and $\sum_{p \in G_{ik}} \pi_{ikp} = 1$.
The aggregate column of family $(i,k)$, cf.\ \eqref{eq:aggregate-column}, is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:family-hull}
\bar b_{ik} = \sum_{p \in G_{ik}} \pi_{ikp}\, b_p
\;\in\;
\operatorname{conv}\bigl\{\, b_p : p \in G_{ik} \,\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
A latent route family is therefore not necessarily one physical path; it is a
feasible convex combination of physical paths of the same OD pair. This is the
mathematically precise meaning of the latent route family.

Let $y_i \ge 0$ collect the flows on the explicit major paths of OD pair $i$
and $s_{ik} \ge 0$ the flow assigned to family $G_{ik}$, subject to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:z-simplex}
\sum_{p \in \mathcal M_i} y_{ip} + \sum_{k=1}^{r_i} s_{ik} = d_i ,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal M_i$ is the major-path set of OD pair $i$. The implied
minor-path flows are $x_p = \pi_{ikp}\, s_{ik}$ for $p \in G_{ik}$, cf.\
\eqref{eq:group-flow}. Stacking $z_i = (y_i, s_i)$ and $z = (z_1, \dots,
z_\ell)$, the reconstruction of the full path-flow vector is linear,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:G-reconstruction}
x = G z ,
\end{equation}
where $G$ contains an identity column for each explicit major path and a
nonnegative convex-combination column (the shares $\pi_{ikp}$) for each latent
route family. The following proposition records the properties that make this
representation exact convex programming rather than approximation heuristics.

\begin{proposition}[Convexity preservation of latent path-space compression]
\label{prop:convexity-preservation}
Let each compressed column of OD pair $i$ be either a feasible path-incidence
vector (a major path) or a convex combination \eqref{eq:family-hull} of
feasible path-incidence vectors of the same OD pair. Then:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] every compressed solution $z \ge 0$ satisfying
\eqref{eq:z-simplex} reconstructs, via \eqref{eq:G-reconstruction}, a
nonnegative path-flow vector satisfying exact OD conservation, so the
represented path-flow set $\mathcal X_G = \{\, Gz : z \in \mathcal Z \,\}$,
with $\mathcal Z = \prod_i \{ z_i \ge 0 : \mathbf 1' z_i = d_i \}$, is a
convex subset of the original feasible path-flow set $\mathcal X$ of problem
\eqref{eq:original_ta};
\item[(b)] if each link travel-time function is nondecreasing, the compressed
problem
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:compressed-fw}
\min_{z \in \mathcal Z} \; f\bigl(\bar B' z + v_0\bigr),
\qquad \bar B = G' B ,
\end{equation}
is a convex program over a product of OD simplices.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) Nonnegativity: $z \ge 0$ and $G \ge 0$ entrywise imply $x = Gz \ge 0$; in
particular $x_p = \pi_{ikp} s_{ik} \ge 0$ for every minor path. Conservation:
for OD pair $i$,
$\sum_{p \in G_{ik}} x_p = s_{ik} \sum_{p \in G_{ik}} \pi_{ikp} = s_{ik}$,
so the total path flow of OD pair $i$ equals
$\sum_{p} y_{ip} + \sum_k s_{ik} = d_i$ by \eqref{eq:z-simplex}. Hence $Gz \in
\mathcal X$. Each $\mathcal Z_i$ is a simplex, so $\mathcal Z$ is convex, and
$\mathcal X_G$ is the linear image of a convex set, hence convex.
(b) The link-flow map $v = B'x + v_0 = B'Gz + v_0 = \bar B' z + v_0$ is affine
in $z$, and $f$ is convex when the link travel-time functions are
nondecreasing; therefore $z \mapsto f(\bar B' z + v_0)$ is convex, and the
constraint set is a product of simplices.
\end{proof}

The method thus solves the original convex assignment problem over a
compressed convex \emph{inner approximation} $\mathcal X_G \subseteq \mathcal
X$ of the path-flow feasible region. Promotion preserves this structure:
when exact pricing identifies a missing path $p^\star$ and appends its
incidence vector as a new identity column of $G$, the represented regions
nest, $\mathcal X_{G^{(0)}} \subseteq \mathcal X_{G^{(1)}} \subseteq \cdots
\subseteq \mathcal X$, every intermediate problem remains convex, and the
restricted optima decrease monotonically toward $f^\star$ --- the standard
column-generation guarantee, inherited without modification because
promotion never leaves the convex-combination framework.

\subsection{The Latent Atom Geometry}
\label{sec:atom-geometry}

For OD pair $i$ with path set $\mathcal P_i$, an \emph{atom} is a fixed
path-flow distribution
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:atom-def}
\pi_{ik} \in \Delta(\mathcal P_i)
= \Bigl\{\pi \ge 0 : \textstyle\sum_{p \in \mathcal P_i} \pi_p = 1\Bigr\},
\qquad k \in \mathcal A_i,
\end{equation}
carrying a nonnegative mass $s_{ik}$. The path and link flows are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:atom-decode}
x_p = \sum_{k \in \mathcal A_i} s_{ik}\, \pi_{ikp},
\qquad
v = \bar v_0 + \sum_i \sum_{k \in \mathcal A_i} s_{ik}\, \bar b_{ik},
\qquad
\bar b_{ik} = \sum_{p} \pi_{ikp}\, b_p,
\end{equation}
with the aggregate columns $\bar b_{ik}$ precomputed as in
\eqref{eq:aggregate-column}, and the masses constrained to the OD demand
simplex
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:atom-simplex}
s_i \in \Delta_{d_i}
= \Bigl\{ s \ge 0 : \textstyle\sum_{k \in \mathcal A_i} s_{ik} = d_i
\Bigr\}.
\end{equation}
Three representation classes are compared. \textbf{E} (explicit): one
singleton atom per path, $|\mathcal A_i| = K$; this is the full path
pool. \textbf{C} (pure latent): $m$ grouped atoms per OD with
nominal-flow shares and no singleton, $|\mathcal A_i| = m \ll K$.
\textbf{RC} (major--latent): one singleton atom on the largest
nominal-flow path plus $m-1$ grouped atoms, the atom form of the
production representation of Section~\ref{sec:grouped}. Throughout this
section $m = 8$.

Two structural facts carry the section. First, feasibility is
inherited, not enforced: every atom is a distribution
\eqref{eq:atom-def}, so by
Proposition~\ref{prop:convexity-preservation} any
$s \in \prod_i \Delta_{d_i}$ decodes to $x \ge 0$ with $Ax = d$ exactly,
and the atom hull is a convex subset of the original feasible set ---
iterates of any feasible method on \eqref{eq:atom-simplex} are feasible
path flows, with no multipliers, no penalties, and no dense inequality
system. Second, the geometry is the one already in the paper: the
anchor-eliminated capped simplex of Remark~\ref{rem:anchor-nonneg} in
the coordinates of Section~\ref{sec:grouped} is \emph{identical} to the
demand simplex \eqref{eq:atom-simplex} over the atom set
$\{\text{anchor singleton}\} \cup \{\text{exported major and group
columns}\}$, since every column contributes exactly its own mass to the
OD total. The six-solver acceptance test below exploits this identity:
one exported representation, several solvers, one optimum.

The latent gradient is the atom cost,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:atom-gradient}
\frac{\partial F}{\partial s_{ik}}
= \sum_{p \in G_{ik}} \pi_{ikp}\, t_p(v)
= \bar b_{ik}^{\,\top}\, t(v),
\end{equation}
one sparse inner product per atom against the precomputed signature,
exactly the online kernel of Section~\ref{sec:compressed-gradients}.
Note the contrast with the reduced coordinates: \eqref{eq:atom-gradient}
uses absolute path costs, not anchor differences; nothing in this
section is a reduced-gradient method, and the anchor-eliminated
representation R of Section~\ref{sec:elimination} remains a separate
representation baseline, not an algorithm.

\subsection{Two Update Operators, One Certificate}
\label{sec:two-operators}

\paragraph{Latent Frank--Wolfe.}
The linear minimization oracle over \eqref{eq:atom-simplex} is
separable and closed-form: for each OD pair, place the full demand on
the atom of minimum cost \eqref{eq:atom-gradient}. This is verbatim the
classical FW shortest-path oracle with ``path'' replaced by ``atom''; on
class E the two coincide. Steps use the exact BPR line search. The FW
duality gap
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fw-gap}
\mathrm{gap}(s)
= \sum_i \Bigl( \textstyle\sum_k s_{ik}\, \bar t_{ik}
  - d_i \min_k \bar t_{ik} \Bigr),
\qquad \bar t_{ik} = \bar b_{ik}^{\,\top} t(v),
\end{equation}
certifies suboptimality over the atom hull.

\paragraph{Latent Projected Gradient.}
The second operator keeps the atoms, the gradient
\eqref{eq:atom-gradient}, the objective, and even the stopping
certificate \eqref{eq:fw-gap}, computable from the identical oracle ---
and changes only the update: a Euclidean projection of a
Barzilai--Borwein trial point onto each demand simplex, followed by
Armijo backtracking along the projected direction. The per-OD
projection is the standard $O(|\mathcal A_i| \log |\mathcal A_i|)$
sorting algorithm. We call the method \emph{Latent Projected Gradient}
(LPG); it is deliberately not called a reduced-gradient method, since it
involves no basis, no elimination, and no reduced costs. A third
operator, latent mirror descent under the entropy geometry
(multiplicative per-OD updates with a step controller and the same
certificate), is included as a robustness check.\footnote{Reference
implementations: \texttt{fw\_origin\_compression.py} (latent FW, exact
BPR line search, origin-block variant),
\texttt{latent\_projected\_gradient.py} (LPG, about $90$ lines on top of
the shared representation layer), and
\texttt{run\_portability\_ext.py} (mirror descent and the extension
cells), all in
\texttt{compressed\_TAP\_repro\_package\_v3\_1\_LPG/repro/fw/src/}; the
C++ projected-gradient kernel on the exported \texttt{group.csv}
representation is \texttt{repro/cpp/tapkernel.cpp}, and the
dependency-free CSR atom kernel is \texttt{repro/fw/cpp/fw\_atom\_kernel.cpp}.}

Sharing the certificate \eqref{eq:fw-gap} across operators is what makes
the comparison well posed: every solver is stopped by the same
computable bound on the same restricted problem.

\subsection{Matched-Accuracy Protocol}
\label{sec:portability-protocol}

The evaluation discipline of Section~\ref{sec:revised-protocol} applies
with one addition. For each representation $\rho$, the restricted
optimum $F^*_\rho$ is first computed to certificate $10^{-10}$. Each
timed run of each solver on $\rho$ then records its
\emph{time-to-target}: the wall time at which its objective first
reaches $F^*_\rho\,(1 + 10^{-6})$. Speedups are within-solver ratios of
times-to-target, $T(\mathrm{E}\text{-}X)/T(\mathrm{RC}\text{-}X)$, so
each solver chases its own representation's optimum to the same relative
accuracy; the representation error
$(F^*_\rho - F^*_{\mathrm E})/F^*_{\mathrm E}$ is reported separately
and is never folded into a speedup. Timing repetitions are one,
single-thread; the deterministic columns (optima, gaps, iteration
counts, feasibility residuals) are exact and reproduce the package's
recorded values across machines.

\paragraph{FW-native results on the real subproblems.}
Latent FW itself, run as the primary solver on the route-rich controlled
ensemble, reproduces the break-even structure of
Section~\ref{sec:grid-two-factor}: grouped atoms accelerate FW by
$1.06 \to 1.45 \to 4.64$ over $K = 8/16/32$ at objective gaps of
$0.19 \to 0.11 \to 0.045\%$, and the origin-block conditional-gradient
variant by $1.20 \to 1.57 \to 6.74$ at the same gaps. The signed SVD
basis is not a valid FW atom --- it violates path nonnegativity and
changes the oracle --- and its correct FW role is feasible path
\emph{screening}: pivoted-QR selection of a restricted path pool, where
flow weighting again dominates its unweighted counterpart
($0.015\%$ vs.\ $0.31\%$ at $K = 16$; $0.020\%$ vs.\ $0.245\%$ at
$K = 32$), the accuracy-per-dimension mechanism of
Section~\ref{sec:grid-two-factor} surviving inside a third algorithmic
context.

\paragraph{The support mechanism, third appearance.}
The regime structure of Section~\ref{sec:major-latent} reappears
unprompted. On the anchor-supported Sioux Falls subproblems every
representation and every solver is exact with near-unit speedups ---
there is nothing to compress away from a one-path support. On the
congested Chicago subproblem the pure-latent class C fails by
$149.8\%$: convex combinations with nominal shares cannot concentrate
mass on the dominant path. One singleton atom per OD (class RC) repairs
this to $0.53\%$, with within-solver E/RC speedups of
$95\times$ (FW) and $4.3\times$ (LPG) on that instance in the recorded
run.
The share-ablation ordering, the oracle bound, and now the atom
geometry give the same verdict three independent ways: the explicit
major is the indispensable degree of freedom, and the latent block is
valuable only on top of it.

\paragraph{Cross-solver, cross-language equivalence.}
The package acceptance test exports one instance through the standard
interface and solves the identical representation six ways: SLSQP on
the reduced coordinates, the C++ Barzilai--Borwein projected gradient
on the capped simplex, Python LPG on the atoms, latent FW on the atoms,
latent mirror descent on the atoms, and FW on the full pool against the
R0 reference. On the sparse instances all methods agree to $10^{-13}$
or better; on the spread-support instance LPG reaches
$4 \times 10^{-16}$ of the C++/SLSQP optimum in $81$ iterations, and
mirror descent $3.3 \times 10^{-13}$ in $588$, while vanilla FW's
sublinear tail is still $7 \times 10^{-5}$ away after $40{,}000$
iterations --- acceptance is gap-certified, so this is recorded as
agreement within FW's certificate, not as failure. On the controlled
ensemble, mirror descent matches LPG to $3 \times 10^{-15}$ at both
$K = 32$ and $K = 512$. The verified equivalence chain of
Section~\ref{sec:revised-protocol} therefore closes as
\begin{equation*}
\text{ALM} \;\approx\; \text{simplex-PG} \;\approx\;
\text{reduced gradient} \;\approx\; \text{latent FW}
\;\approx\; \text{LPG} \;\approx\; \text{latent MD},
\end{equation*}
in two languages, in both support regimes, through one interchange
format.

\subsection{Beyond Traffic: Major--Latent Prototypes for General Column Pools}
\label{sec:beyond-traffic}

Nothing in Proposition~\ref{prop:convexity-preservation} is specific to
traffic assignment: the argument uses only that the decision vector is a
nonnegative combination of columns, that the observable quantity is linear in
the decisions, and that the objective is convex in the observable. Two small
prototypes\footnote{\texttt{major\_latent\_prototypes/} (LP-relaxation
prototypes, \texttt{examples/run\_all.py}); deterministic column counts and
relative errors verified by a fresh run on 2026-07-18. The prototypes
deliberately exclude branch-and-price, integer recovery, and software
integration; they test only the representation layer.} exercise the same
representation, $x = Wz$ with effective matrix $\widetilde B = BW$ and cost
$\widetilde c = W' c$ (the operator $W$ is the general-column-pool analogue of
$G$ in \eqref{eq:G-reconstruction}), on classical column-pool linear programs:
cutting stock, where the original columns are cutting patterns, and unit
commitment, where the original columns are feasible generator schedules. Each
prototype compares the full explicit pool (E), fully latent convex atoms with
no explicit major columns (C), and explicit majors plus latent minor atoms
(RC).

\subsection{What the Portability Result Licenses}
\label{sec:portability-conclusions}

The section's contribution is a boundary statement. The latent
route-family representation --- explicit majors plus nonnegative grouped
atoms --- can be dropped into any first-order traffic-assignment method
whose feasible geometry is the product of OD demand simplices:
Frank--Wolfe and its origin-block variants, projected gradient, and
entropic mirror methods, with the atom oracle, the simplex projection,
and the multiplicative update as the respective one-line substitutions.
The gain is governed by $K/m$ and by support coverage, not by the choice
of operator; the FW-specific machinery of the literature is not what
carries it. Signed spectral bases participate through screening, not as
atoms. And because the interchange format stores atoms as ordered arc
sequences, the $K/m \sim 10^2$ regime where the speedup is largest is
precisely the regime of space-time column pools, where each arc is a
(link, departure-time) pair and nothing in the geometry, the operators,
or the certificate changes. That extension is designed into the
interface and is deliberately left as the next piece of work rather
than claimed here.

\subsection{Summary Statement}
\label{sec:fw-origin-summary}

The latent route-family representation is convexity preserving. Each latent
column is constructed as a nonnegative convex combination of feasible path
columns within the same OD pair. The compressed decision variables remain on
the OD demand simplex, and their linear reconstruction produces nonnegative
path flows satisfying exact OD conservation. Consequently, the represented
feasible set is a convex subset of the original path-flow polytope. Because
link flows are linear in the compressed variables and the Beckmann potential
is convex under nondecreasing link costs, the resulting compressed assignment
remains a convex program. Standard Frank--Wolfe updates retain their classical
interpretation as convex combinations of feasible assignments. This is a
central methodological property of the framework, not an implementation
detail: it is what allows the compression to be embedded in FW, origin-based,
and column-generation solvers without altering their convergence theory.
